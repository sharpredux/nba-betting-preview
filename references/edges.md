# Market comparison and qualitative edge tiers

## Establish the actual bet

Match game identity, date, period, settlement rules where material, side, number, and price. Default to full-game markets; do not compare a first-half quote with a full-game statistic or mix alternate lines with the main line. Preserve spread signs and name the team on every recommended side. Use American odds by default, while retaining the user's supplied format if clearer. Do not silently assume -110.

For consensus context, prefer a board with multiple named bookmaker quotes. Compare it with a second accessible market source when possible. Report a representative **observed quote** and material book-to-book range rather than averaging lines and attaching a price that was never available. Distinguish bookmaker variation from a conflict between sources. If only one provider is available, label that limitation.

Examples of clear selections: `Team A +4.5 (-110)`, `Under 228.5 (-108)`, `Team B ML (+125)`. These are formatting examples, not current prices. A posted spread without juice is incomplete for a value claim. If a key quote or price is missing, mark that market as pass/pending; an explicitly unpriced basketball observation can still be useful.

Opening versus current lines can describe movement only when both are sourced for the same market with times. Movement alone does not establish informed money, and stale opening value is not an available current bet. Consensus snapshots do not establish availability at the user's sportsbook.

## Compare evidence with price

Assess spread, total, and moneyline internally before choosing the game's best angle. Ask which factors the market likely already reflects, what evidence suggests remaining value **at this quote**, and what argues against the selection. A likely winner is not automatically a good moneyline bet, nor does a rest advantage alone justify laying a spread.

For totals, combine pace and scoring efficiency with opponent defense, shot profile, personnel, and overtime/variance context. Simply adding the teams' PPG, averaging recent final scores, or summing missing players' PPG does not produce a fair total. Treat any unsupported precise projection as unavailable rather than manufacturing one.

Rest, travel, schedule density, and fatigue overlap. Injury effects can already appear in recent performance and odds. Avoid counting correlated observations as independent evidence. Explain why a factor may still matter after accounting for the market rather than assigning rigid weights or point adjustments.

## Rating criteria

These tiers describe the quality and coherence of the argument. They are not calibrated probabilities, measured expected value, or a promise of profitability. Use no quotas for picks or tier labels.

| Tier | When to use it |
| --- | --- |
| **Pass** | No clear value at the available quote; crucial price/data is missing or stale; contradictory evidence dominates; or material unresolved news could reverse the selection. State the main reason or the condition to revisit. |
| **Lean** | A directional case exists at a known quote, but support is thin, noisy, or vulnerable to a substantial counterargument. Treat as a watchlist item. |
| **Moderate** | Current market and core facts are verified; distinct relevant factors support a plausible pricing mismatch; the strongest counterargument is addressed; and no unresolved issue is likely to reverse the case. |
| **Strong** | Meets Moderate criteria with unusually coherent evidence, corroborated critical facts, limited material uncertainty, and a clear explanation of why the current price may understate the case. Reserve this tier; qualitative confidence still does not prove a profitable edge. |

Separate uncertainty about data from uncertainty about the matchup. Missing injury data cannot make a known player absence disappear, and a verified report cannot make a game outcome certain. Without a validated model, do not generate numeric cover/win probabilities, percentage edges, precise fair lines, confidence percentages, or EV figures. Market-implied probability, if specifically requested, is a price conversion and must not be presented as the analyst's forecast.

For a questionable high-impact player, give concise in/out scenarios. If one scenario favors a different side or materially changes the total case, use **Pass — awaiting status** for the affected market. Another market can still qualify if the uncertainty does not undermine its reasoning.

## Ranking and price sensitivity

Rank supported candidates by evidence quality, uncertainty, and the comparison with their quoted prices. Each ranked entry needs its selection/price, tier, supporting case, best counterargument, and invalidation condition. If only leans exist, label the list as a watchlist and say that no supported betting edge clears the bar. Never promote a weak candidate just to produce a “best bet.”

A recommendation applies to its quoted number and price at the stated time. If giving a different playable limit, explain its basis; do not invent a precise cutoff to imitate model output. Otherwise instruct reassessment after a material line/price change. A worse number or more expensive price can erase the original case.

User-supplied lines take priority for their analysis, but note if their timestamp or current availability is unknown. Keep them separate from sourced market snapshots. State explicitly if the user's different quote changes the ranking or makes the angle a pass. Do not recommend parlays, stake sizes, loss recovery, or guaranteed outcomes as part of this workflow.
